# Medication Reconciliation Protocol

> **SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT.** Written for a fictional organization ("Maple Ridge Health System").
> Not clinical guidance. Contains no real patient data.

## Purpose

Medication reconciliation reduces errors at transitions of care by comparing the medications a
patient was taking before admission with the medications ordered in the hospital and at discharge.

## Best Possible Medication History

A best possible medication history is obtained within 24 hours of admission. It uses at least two
sources, for example a patient or caregiver interview plus a pharmacy fill history or the list
from the patient's primary care clinic. Pharmacy technicians trained in medication history may
collect the history; a pharmacist or prescriber verifies it.

## Reconciliation Points

Reconciliation is required at three points: admission, transfer between levels of care (for
example from the intensive care unit to a medical unit), and discharge.

## Discrepancies

Any unintentional discrepancy, such as an omitted home medication or a wrong dose, is
communicated to the prescriber and resolved before the next scheduled dose. Intentional changes
must have a documented reason in the medication record.

## High-Alert Medications

Anticoagulants, insulin, and opioids are high-alert medications. Any change to a high-alert
medication at discharge requires an independent double check by a second clinician and specific
teaching for the patient.

## Patient Communication

At discharge, the patient receives a single reconciled medication list that marks each medication
as new, changed, continued, or stopped. Patients are instructed to bring this list to their
follow-up appointment.
