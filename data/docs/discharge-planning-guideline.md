# Discharge Planning Guideline

> **SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT.** Written for a fictional organization ("Maple Ridge Health System").
> Not clinical guidance. Contains no real patient data.

## Purpose

This guideline describes how inpatient care teams at the fictional Maple Ridge Health System
prepare patients for a safe transition out of the hospital.

## Timing

Discharge planning begins at admission. The assigned case manager completes the initial
discharge needs assessment within 24 hours of admission, or within 48 hours for weekend
admissions. The estimated discharge date is recorded in the care plan by the end of hospital day 2.

## Roles

The attending physician confirms medical readiness for discharge. The case manager coordinates
post-acute services, transportation, and equipment. The bedside nurse delivers discharge education
using the teach-back method and documents that the patient or caregiver can restate the key instructions.

## Discharge Checklist

Before the patient leaves the unit, the team confirms that:

- the discharge medication list has been reconciled and reviewed with the patient;
- a follow-up appointment is scheduled within 7 days for high-risk patients;
- durable medical equipment has been delivered or confirmed for delivery;
- the written after-visit summary is provided in the patient's preferred language.

## High-Risk Criteria

A patient is considered high risk for readmission when any of the following apply: two or more
admissions in the previous 6 months, a new diagnosis of heart failure, more than 10 scheduled
medications, or lives alone without a caregiver. High-risk patients receive a pharmacist
consultation before discharge.

## Discharge Before Noon

The target is for 40 percent of planned discharges to leave the unit before 12:00 noon.
Discharge orders for next-day discharges should be written the evening before whenever possible.
