# Language Access and Interpreter Services Policy

> **SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT.** Written for a fictional organization ("Maple Ridge Health System").
> Not legal guidance. Contains no real patient data.

## Policy Statement

Patients with limited English proficiency and patients who are deaf or hard of hearing are offered
qualified interpreter services at no cost, for all clinical encounters.

## Qualified Interpreters

Only qualified medical interpreters may interpret clinical information. Family members, and
especially minors, should not be used as interpreters except in an emergency until a qualified
interpreter is available. Bilingual staff may communicate directly with patients only after passing
the organization's language proficiency assessment.

## Modalities

Interpreter services are available in person, by video remote interpreting, and by telephone.
In-person interpreters are preferred for informed consent discussions, family meetings about goals
of care, and discharge teaching for complex regimens. Video interpreting is available 24 hours a day.

## Documentation

The patient's preferred language and the need for an interpreter are recorded at registration.
Each interpreted encounter documents the interpreter's identification number and the modality used.

## Translated Materials

Vital documents, including consent forms and discharge instructions, are provided in the five most
common non-English languages of the service area. For other languages, a qualified interpreter
provides sight translation.
